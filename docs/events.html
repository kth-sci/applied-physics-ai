Session 1 — Tools and foundations

State-of-the-art tools, GitHub for people who do not use it daily, running local models on the department's DGX Spark, and how to write an agent skill. A printed cheat sheet goes out as a pre-read so the hour is not spent on setup.

Session 2 — Experience sharing

What people have actually used AI for over the past year, including what did not work — pitfalls are at least as useful as successes. Pre-work: bring one concrete case to share.

Session 3 — Agentic mini hackathon (4 h)

The centrepiece, in groups that deliberately mix newcomers with experienced users. Candidate topics: a research agent, a grading assistant, a teaching assistant, an email-crunching agent, and processing sensitive data with a local model on the DGX Spark. Roughly six per group; every group commits at least one SKILLS.md to the shared repository. A $3,000 API token budget is allocated per group.

Session 4 — Ethics and departmental guidelines

Case-study discussion: responsible use in teaching and learning, how far AI should go in lab reports and thesis writing, who owns data shared with an AI system, whether an AI-generated idea can be patented, and the human–AI working relationship. It closes the retreat deliberately — this is the session that produces the guidelines, editing a real draft rather than a blank page.
